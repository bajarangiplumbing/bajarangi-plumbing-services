{% comment %} ---------- [6] LOCAL PROOF ---------- {% endcomment %} {% comment %} FABRICATED REVIEWS REMOVED - compliance review, September 2026. Each of the six service pages used to override a `svc_proof` block here with two
testimonials: five gold stars, a quote, and an attribution reading "Placeholder name · ". Twelve in total, plus three more on the home page. Every one was invented. Why a "Sample" tag was not a sufficient defence: - Consumer Protection Act 2019 s.2(28) defines a misleading advertisement to include one that gives a false description or a false guarantee. A fabricated endorsement is exactly that, and the CCPA can penalise it up to Rs 10 lakh, rising to Rs 50 lakh for a repeat. The tag was rendered at .6rem in #9AA5B4 on a pale yellow card - roughly 2:1 contrast, the least legible text on the page - so the disclosure was materially harder to read than the claim it was qualifying. - IS 19000:2022, the BIS standard on online consumer reviews, requires a published review to be traceable to a real, verifiable author. - The markup was semantic
and with a locality attribution, so a search engine, an AI summariser or a screen reader encountered a genuine-looking review. The stars were aria-hidden, so a screen-reader user got the testimonial without even the visual cue that it was a sample. The bar before anything goes back here: a real, named customer who has given written permission for their words to be published. When that exists, add Review / AggregateRating JSON-LD in base.html at the same time - it is deliberately absent today, and it must stay absent while there is nothing true to put in it. The photograph placeholders below stay. They promise nothing and claim nothing; they are visibly empty slots, not invented content. {% endcomment %}

These slots are empty on purpose. Genuine photographs of my own jobs go here once I have the customer's permission to publish them — I would rather show you nothing than show you someone else's stock photograph.
